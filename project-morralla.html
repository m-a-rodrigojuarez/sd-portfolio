Walmart · Service Design

Structuring Walmart's Store Cash Logistics

Service blueprinting and requirements definition for Treasury Operations at scale

We mapped how cash moved between SuperCenter stores, transport providers, and the bank to turn inconsistent manual routines into documented, auditable operating flows.

01

Context

Walmart's Treasury Operations team wanted to reduce the uncertainty around how SuperCenter stores handled physical cash, from the moment it entered the register to the moment it reached the bank vault, and back. This work was part of a broader initiative, internally known as Show Me the Money, split into two complementary flows: cash leaving the store to be deposited at the bank, and cash arriving at the store from the bank to fund daily operations.

02

Problem

Neither flow had a standardized process. Steps varied store by store, depended heavily on physical paperwork, and were executed manually with no shared source of truth between store managers, cash transport providers, and the bank. That variability made it difficult for Treasury to forecast, audit, or trust the cash-handling process at scale.

03

My role

I led the UX research and service design workstream, managing a researcher and a contractor service designer, and worked directly with Treasury and the platform vendor to translate what we found into requirements they could build.

04

Process

We mapped both flows end to end through workshops with Treasury, store visits, and interviews with store managers and cash transport staff. From that, we built As-Is blueprints, designed To-Be flows with every team involved, and translated the future state into BRDs for the vendor backlog.

05

Outcome

The work translated into two BRDs with ten requirements added to the platform vendor's backlog, alongside a new transport receipt document that gave Treasury a documented, auditable process to replace an inconsistent, paper-dependent one.

Operational frictions

Store-by-store variability

Each store handled cash routines differently, making the process hard to forecast or audit.

Paper-dependent evidence

Forms, signatures, and physical artifacts carried key proof across multiple handoff points.

No shared source of truth

Stores, transport providers, Treasury, and the bank worked from fragmented process visibility.

Service design outputs

As-Is and To-Be blueprints

The flows documented how cash moved today and defined the future operating model for both cash directions.

Vendor-ready BRDs

The blueprints were translated into requirements the external platform vendor could add to its backlog.

Proof-of-custody receipt

We proposed and designed a signed receipt to close the custody gap during cash transport pickup.
