About me

Researcher by practice, product and technology by background.

Hi, I'm Rodrigo, a Product & UX Researcher based in Ensenada, Baja California. My background spans research, product management, and software development, so I tend to look at problems from more than just the user side. I'm interested in how people, operations, business needs, and technology come together, especially when the problem isn't clearly defined yet.

Most recently at Walmart Mexico, I led research across eCommerce and transportation products, working with Product, Design, Operations, and business teams on problems ranging from onboarding and last-mile operations to store cash logistics. Before that, I worked in UX consulting at Sperientia and spent five years at Hoteles City Express, where I moved from software development into project and product leadership.

I also teach quantitative UX methods at Universidad ICESI and advise graduate research. Teaching keeps me close to the fundamentals of evidence and measurement, while my engineering background keeps me asking whether an idea can actually work in the real world.

Outside work, you'll usually find me playing football, training CrossFit, or enjoying life in Ensenada.

How I work

Start with the problem, not the method.

I don't start with a favorite framework or research method. I start by understanding the decision we need to make, what we don't know yet, and what evidence would help us move forward.

Frame complex problems

I look beyond the immediate user experience to understand the workflows, constraints, stakeholders, and business context shaping the problem.

Build the right evidence

I choose qualitative, quantitative, behavioral, or operational evidence based on the question, and combine them when one source isn't enough.

Turn evidence into direction

I bring teams into the process to make sense of what we learn, explore options, and turn the evidence into priorities, requirements, or product decisions.

Publications

Effect of technology on aging perception

Technology can support active aging, but its impact on aging perception depends on older adults’ prior attitudes toward their own aging process. This study found that technology use can influence perceived aging and led to a sociotechnical model connecting technology use with successful aging.

View publication
Do technology-related stimuli affect age estimation?

The study explored whether exposure to technology-related concepts changes aging perception through age estimation. Results suggested that actual technology use, studied longitudinally with older adults, may be more relevant for understanding how technology influences aging perception.

View publication
Mental models, performance and usability of a complex interactive system: the case of Twitter

This study analyzed how people’s mental models and expertise relate to Twitter usability and performance. Results showed that expertise had a stronger impact on performance, while both expertise and mental model style influenced perceived usability.

View publication
weRemember: Letting AD patients to enjoy their home and their families

weRemember is a prototype that supports people with Alzheimer’s in maintaining independence at home and communicating with family. Iterative evaluations suggested positive effects on caregiver relationships and the home experience.
